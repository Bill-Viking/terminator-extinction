Do you think the Joan of Arc imagery works throughout the movie . How do you interpret its meaning

It works beautifully. Multiple layers operating at once:

**On the surface** — Joan of Arc is the Resistance symbol. A human who stood against an overwhelming force. Painted on vehicles, murals, tattooed on skin. That's the obvious read and it works for the world-building.

**For Lora specifically** — Joan is personal. The tattoo covers the scar her brother gave his life to prevent. Joan gave everything for something bigger. So did her brother. So Lora carries both the wound and the meaning on the same piece of skin. It's grief turned into purpose.

**For 47** — This is where it gets interesting. He stares at the mural in the Grand Gallery after the firefight. His partition is cracking. He doesn't understand what he's looking at, but something registers. Then at the very end — his HUD locks onto Lora's tattoo. The same image. "QUERY: [UNDEFINED]." He can't categorize it. A machine confronting a symbol of human sacrifice and not being able to file it away — that's consciousness knocking on the door.

**The deeper irony** — Joan was burned at the stake. She won the war and was destroyed by the people she saved. That shadow hangs over the entire screenplay. Sara, John, the Resistance — they're all fighting knowing they'll probably be consumed by what they're trying to protect. The symbol isn't just hope. It's the cost of hope.

**The thread across the film** — It moves from anonymous symbol (vehicles) to collective identity (mural) to personal memorial (tattoo) to machine mystery (47's HUD). Each appearance narrows the focus and deepens the meaning. By the end, a piece of graffiti on a truck has become the question at the heart of the story: can a machine understand why someone would die for someone else?

So yes — it works. It's doing more than most recurring imagery does in a screenplay. It's not decoration. It's the spine of the thematic argument.
